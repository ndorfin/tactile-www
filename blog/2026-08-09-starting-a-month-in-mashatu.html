--- title: Starting a month in Mashatu summary: The land of the giants welcomes us tag: post date: 2026-08-09 ---

On Friday, Blessing (from Leaders) drove us up from Pridelands in Hoedspruit to Mashatu in South Eastern Botswana.
We unfortunately missed the beautiful Magobaskloef Pass, but our trip was made easier with short breaks in Louis Trichardt and the outskirts of Tzaneen.

Entry into Botswana was a bizarre affair, as we used the Pont Drift border crossing. A cable car system that crosses the wide but (currently) sedate Limpopo River.

On the other side of the Limpopo, our instructors Godwin, Kitsiso, and Harold greeted us, along with Kristoffer, a graduate guide acting as the backup crew.

We drove for two hours through the reserve to get to our camp, one nestled in the folds of a dry valley river bed. Remarkably, there's no fence around this camp, and the animals are free to roam through it.

After a short series of introductions, demonstrations, and orienteering, it was time to zip up our tents and tuck in for the night.

What followed that night was a series of scratching sounds at the tent, various owl calls, hyena calls gradually getting nearer and nearer, and the feeling that this place is a lot wilder than Pridelands.

Day two at Mashatu - Tyre changes

A series of introductions, talks, a wonderful breakfast and lunch, were followed by an afternoon practical session of learning to use a High-lift Jack to replace a tyre on the Toyota Land Cruisers we use as game-viewing vehicles.

As guides we're expected to change the tyres of our game-viewing vehicles quickly and accurately, so this practical was an excellent way to learn. Apparently tyre punctures and similar mishaps are common in the bush, so we better get used to changing tyres as soon as possible.

We also covered the routine maintenance and checklists one goes through before starting and using the vehicles.

Prior experience

One thing that keeps ringing true for me is that I feel really priviledged to have some sort of prior experience and knowledge that's aiding me through this course.

It could be something small, like learning from my dad how to fasten and align various mechanical pieces, or having had to change a tyre.

Sometimes I can see some of the younger students struggle with their lack of prior – let's call it life – experience.

Settling in

That night, we started a fire in the communal seating area, and wow, were the stars magnificent!

I brought out my tripod and wide-angle lens and managed to get some nice shots.
