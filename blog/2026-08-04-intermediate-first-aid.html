--- title: Intermediate first aid summary: Safety first, right? tag: post date: 2026-08-04 ---

Andrew, has literally been through the wars. A man seemingly in his 60s - grey hair, a smoker's voice - with the younger man's energy and passion about his work as a paramedic, business owner, and part-time lecturer.

His lecturing style is based on reinforcement, effective shock tactics, and intense personal engagement of his students.

We completed three to four days of theory work in the lecture hall, but boy, was it fascinating. Andrew kept us eganged, but wow, did I feel grateful for having watched both seasons of The Pitt - the amount of medical terms thrown around were staggering.

Wednesday's CPR practical was visceral. CPR is tiring! And we had the luxury of working on a clean and cool lecture room floor. Imagine having to do CPR in the thorny bush, with the added threat of wild animals around us.

We also covered a practical in choking, and how best to help someone, even if they're a baby. And nothing will make us feel more helpless that an obstructed airway and no way to clear it.

Learning through doing

On Thursday we had an intense exercise where we simulated a bush-based incident; our poor practice dummy Bob had multiple open wounds, an impalement, and a burn. Our supplied first aid kit was deliberately sparse in key ingredients, so we had to make do, on the scene, with what we had on our person.

Our team of 11 split into watchers and carers.
The watchers set up a perimeter and kept an eye out for wildlife. While the carers got down and started attending to Bob.

I managed to successful pierce both sets of my gloves while handling safety pins (damn, they're finnicky), but thankfully we had enough of us for someone to step in and carry on

We worked the problem, and eventually had Bob on a spine board, only realising while doing so that we didn't attend to his cervical spine injury. We probalby did more damage than good, but that's why we do such exercises; to better our awareness and skills.

Then the walk back to camp carrying Bob, escorted by our armed guides.

Ill-preparedness

One of the sad or harsh realities you'll realise in doing such a course, is that most venues, organisations, and operators don't have enough first aid equipment, nor is it part of their consciousness. There are so many first aid devices and tools that cost comparitvely less than their usual infrastructure or supplies, and yet can be instrumental in saving a life. A good example would be an AED, critical for cardiac arrest, and yet a fraction of the cost of a game viewing vehcile.

One of the possible reasons we don't see such good first aid equipment coverage is because it's not mandated by law. The minimum requirements are so low, that it's almost laughable.
