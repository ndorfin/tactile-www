--- title: Compression summary: tag: post date: 2026-09-19 ---

All the field observations we've made over the last few weeks.
All the lectures we've attended and learned from.
All the birds and plants and poops and signs we've encountered.
These are all contributing to a foundation that is essential, but will be summarised in just a few words or sentences should we deem it appropriate, timely, and of course relevant to the guests.

Up until now, we've been trained to stop at a sighting and tell the guests something interesting about what we're seeing. To interpret the sighting.

All this time, it's been on easy mode.

Hard mode approaches

We have a week until our practical assessments start.
These assessments are a simulated guest expereince we have to manage from top to bottom; briefings, catering, vehicle readiness, route planning, latest sightings, and of course the actual drive in the reserve.

But the more I think about it, the more I think about how there's little overlap with how we've been training, how our assessments need to be conducted, and how a true guiding experience at a lodge will go.
These all suit different purposes.

Take for instance the practical assessment. We need to include aspects of the local climate, history, biome,and conservation.
We need to be able to verbally identify at least 10 trees, talk about the cultural/medicinal uses of them.
Similar story with the birds: 30 species
5 tracks and signs
et cetera.

Each utterance is meant to flow, where we link the subject in front of us with things we've seen a few minutes prior, or are bound to see later, so that the drive has a theme or congruence to it. It's got to be as interesting to as many of the guests onboard as possible.

And we have to do this ideally while driving, and looking for new sightings, else we'll quickly run out of time and patience to cover everything we need to within the 3 hours or so we have available.

I have one more rehearsal drive before the practical assessment. Wish me luck.

Changes

We've had two instructors go on leave, and two new assessors cum instructors join us.

The two new assessors – a couple – are veteran guides and instructors with almost 70 years of industry experience combined.

Their knowledge is extraordinary, and the way they share it is equally impressive. And even though they have so much experience, they're still learning things everyday, including from us.

We've had to unlearn a few things though, but the new lessons have been great, when they're not overwhelming.
