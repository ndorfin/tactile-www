--- title: Birding summary: Almost done with the Basic Birding course at EcoTraining tag: post date: 2026-10-18 ---

It's been four days in Karongwe Nature Reserve, where EcoTraining has a camp set up in the south of the reserve. We're here for the Basic Birding course.

Ayden (a fellow student) and I spent 2 days driving up from Cape Town, a journey of 1,800km, so we were understandably shattered for the first day of the course.

That said, it's been an amazing course so far, and I wish we had done this course before starting our 55-day NQF2 training; it would have helped the other students so much.

How it's going

Collectively, we've seen and heard 119 bird species over the last 4 days, including some new lifers for me.

We've had daily lectures learning about bird identification, anatomy, conservation projects, citizen science projects, etc.

It's been really hot though. Peaking at 38 degrees this week, with higher temperatures expected next week, which is bad timing as we're doing track & sign courses next. No rain in sight either.

One of the highlights so far has been seeing this rare pale morph Wahlberg's Eagle, it's partner was nesting nearby.

Karongwe

The nature reserve is pristine though. Since the elephant population is strictly controlled, it's such a wonder to see trees in their full splendour, and as such, the bird life is incredible. [There are almost 300 species of birds in this area.]

We've also seen some rare sights one wouldn't normally see, such as the elusive African Wild Cat, and a Black-headed Oriole on its nest.

Tomorrow we're doing the related exams, including a field observation test, and visual ID and call ID tests at the lecture hall.

Coming up…

We'll be doing Track, Sign, and Trails training over the next two weeks. It's going to be intense in this heat.
