--- title: Birding Bonanza summary: A morning dedicated to birding tag: post date: 2026-09-27 ---

Early yesterday morning, 5 of us set out on a planned birding drive through the concession, in what can only be described as a bonanza of sightings.

We left shortly before sunrise, and drove west to the Klaserie River.

Brian did the driving, and I was armed with my camera and the BirdLasser app (which we used to log sightings of each species as we saw or heard them). At times I struggled juggling the app and my camera, as I tried to take photos and keep an accurate log of each species.

The first 30 minutes or so were the most intense, as we trained our ears to the various calls, while trying to identify the visuals in front of us.

Ironically, we didn't see many waders or waterfowl down by the river, but the flowering and fruiting trees along its banks were havens for sunbirds, and fructivores. The insectivores, and swallows were also in their numbers, allowing us to climb to the mid-60s in good time.

As the road ran out along the Klaserie River, we were greeted by a Klaas's Cuckoo

One particular stop involved a Burchell's Coucal that wasn't too shy, it scrambling through a nearby bush looking for food.

…and some Lesser Striped Swallows resting in the morning sun near a culvert.

An Ashy Flycatcher being all cute.

As we entered the eastern side of the concession, we encountered a massive herd of Cape Buffaloes. The Angry Cows were pretty placid, and we were having a field day trying to spot the rarer Yellow-billed Oxpeckers amongst the much larger beasts.

One of the larger woodpeckers, a Bearded Woodpecker sunning itself. They often make tapping sounds on the wood for territorial reasons.

A Burchell's Starling's feathers are irridescent at certain angles.

A Brown-headed Parrot crawling up the underside of a Baobab tree.

A male White-bellied Sunbird glistening in the light.

All in all, we covered 4 pentads, and saw 105 bird species in just under 4 hours.
