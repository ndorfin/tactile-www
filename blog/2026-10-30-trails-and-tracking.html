--- title: Trails and tracking summary: It feels good to be out in the bush again tag: post date: 2026-10-30 ---

The magic of being in the bush faded last week, but this week it's been a bit better as we started doing 'trailing' or 'tracking', and spent less time on the specifics of each animal print.

Each morning and afternoon we'd set out to find a large animal print (such as a Rhino or Buffalo), and then one of us would get assigned to track the animal as it had travelled through the bush.

We have to not only control and engage the rest of the group, but follow the animal successfully. And that quickly proved impossible: our eyes are definitely not as tuned as our instructors and backups, so we're all having to swallow our pride in front of the group and entertain everyone through an amateur guided tracking experience.

We've had to learn to look for signs such as foot scuffing, overturned leaves, scratches on trees, broken branches, regurgitated food, fresh dung, and more.

It has also been a bit cooler than the week before, and we have some idle time as each 'guide' takes their time. This has allowed us to see many interesting arthropods, flowers, and listen to the birds.

Testing our trailing

This morning we started the assessments, after what felt like a rather short week of instruction. [Honestly, we could do with at least another month of training.] So far it's been a slog, and each student is struggling with their confidence and lack of experience.

Next week

On the 2nd, we're off to Makuleke in the far northern section of Kruger for our Advanced Rifle Handling training and assessments. It's surely going to be a watershed moment for a lot of us.
