--- title: Reality check summary: Trying to see the wood for the trees tag: post date: 2026-09-07 ---

It's days like this that make me want reflect on the last month or so, and feel a bit unsure, maybe even a bit hopeless.

It was a tough day, hosting back-to-back game drive sessions, on a cold and rainy day, in a burnt and somewhat barren concession that we've already circled at least twice in the last week.

Pushed over trees in our path, no game, a flat tyre, and gloomy weather all forming a perfect storm of little to see, and the pressure of having to 'interpret' whatever I could after taking the twisty turns again in what felt like a fever dream.

Added to the mix: high school-level drama from the younger ones in the group, and the clash of international cultural differences coming to the fore.

Maybe it's cabin fever

I get the feeling we're all getting a little stir-crazy after having spent a month with complete strangers, and having to work each other out, and fit into the social order. Where does an older man fit in with a group half his age, and a different set of sensibilities?

I think this is somewhat complicated by the fact that half the group doesn't actually seem that interested in pursuing careers as game guides, and this is all rather an elaborate holiday, or a place their parents sent them to in the hopes that they'll find their calling.

Sometimes it even feels like we've had the spoiled rich kids dumped on us.

Regardless, I'm glad this day is behind me, and tomorrow I have the pressure lifted somewhat.

An observation

I think back to my life a year ago, and how dulled my senses were… Listening to music to drown out chit-chat. Staring at a screen all day. Becoming overly familiar with scents that we continuously expereince each day.

Now, I don't think I've ever used all my senses so fully for this amount of time before.

Each day is a barrage of sights, sounds, smells, and sometimes taste, and we're training to focus on these senses actively and intensely. Tuning those sensory organs is brutal, but also feels a little more in tune with how we were.

I'm still struggling to find animals amongst the noisy bush, but the smells and sounds have been fascinating so far.
