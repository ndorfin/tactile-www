--- title: Full day activity summary: Sunburn, smiles and many photos tag: post date: 2026-08-18 ---

Today was a welcome break from the intense routine; an activity which involved extending a morning drive into a whole-day affair.

We drove East, right into the narrowest part of the Tuli Block, to where the South African, Zimbabwean, and Botswana borders meet.

On the way, we stopped for breakfast alongside the Shashe River. Sycamore Figs, Mashatu Trees and tall Lala Palms greeted us with an entourage of fruit-eating birds, Baboons, Palm Swifts, and White-backed Vultures. The distant sound of cowbells chimed out from the Zim side of the river.

The birdlife was especially rich; all the usual suspects were there, but we also had some close encounters with Bee-eaters, African Hawk Eagles, and Kori Bustards

Just before lunch, we arrived at the Limpopo and Shashe River junctions near Coetsers Island, a place steeped in history and furrowed by the two rivers.

After our packed lunch, we walked across the Shashe floodplain to where the river trickled further South, where we could see hundreds of Zimbabwean cows; fuzzy in the heat haze, and the various nation flags positioned atop the hills on the other sides of their respective valleys.

Upon returning to our vehicles, we headed upriver along the Botswana side of the Limpopo, where we saw Crocodiles, a Hippo or two, and all the magical riverine birds one could expect.

Evidence of the Limpopo bursting its banks two years prior was still lingering, but not in physical damage, but rather the invasive weeds and disturbed ground that stretched quite far inland.

Having spent a day on Grasses this last week was especially prescient, as all the pioneer species we learned about were evident in the floodzone.

After two hours and one set of sunburnt legs later, we were back home (camp), enjoying a traditional South African dinner, and good comraderie.
