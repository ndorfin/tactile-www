That's a wrap of the open book, written theory, and species identification tests.

Open book and written theory exam

The 90 minute EcoTraining open book test was a nice way to learn, and prepare us for the FGASA written theory exam. The theory exam ended up being much more interpretive than I expected. Thankfully, I still passed.

Species identification

Today, we had all the EcoTraining species identification tests, which involved a series of presentation slides with a photo (or even calls in some cases) of each species of plant and animal we should know from our general lists.
The bird IDs, their calls, and nests were relatively straight-forward, but I found the Grass ID slides to be really difficult and had to rewrite.

That's it for all the NQF2 theory now, the entry-level qualification.

On to the Practical

On the 2nd I have a morning drive booked for my practical assesment. It's a 3-4 hour drive in which I not only have to be a good host, but also have informative and interesting things to say about the local human history, conservation in general, ecology, weather/climate and geology. Then I also need to identify 30 birds visually, or by call, 10 trees, 5 tracks/signs, and more.

It's going to be intense. Any species I miss or fail to reach in the quota will be tested at a later stage. And the weather is looking gloomy too.
