--- title: A fortnight review summary: What's been happening over the last 12 days tag: post date: 2026-08-24 ---

The last 12 days have seen us pick up the pace not only in lectures, but in practicals too.

Each one of us has been running guiding sessions both on foot and in the game viewing vehicles, while still maintaining the courseload of lectures and self-study.

It's been really interesting watching it all unfold, and sometimes unravel, but we all seem to be getting back up, keep trying, and improving as guides.

The schedule

We've been sticking to twice-daily sessions of one walk and one game drive, but these days we were the ones practicing our guiding under the instructor's supervision, subjecting our teammates to our own interpretative performances of 'guiding'.

The midday is dedicated to a 60 to 90 minute lecture, followed by self-study, log books, and filling in our workbooks. Having done the theory for such things as trees, geology, and mammals, has been wonderful for cementing our in-the-field knowledge of our environs.

During Breakfast and Lunch, the designated student guides have to run briefings for their upcoming sessions, which have been a great opportunity to practice our burgeoning hospitality skills.

My first foray into guiding

I decided to make my first guided walk bird-themed, as I felt reasonably comfortable focusing on birds.

So on a crisp morning over a week ago, we set out in one of the game viewing vehicles to a nice spot near 'Bryce's Store' on the Pitsane river.

After disembarking from the vehicle, I had to give a short briefing on the walking protocol, emphasizing safety, instructions, and our plan.

I talked briefly about the wonderful Mashatu Tree we parked under, and then the Instructor Kitsiso threw me a curveball and asked me to talk about some of the paw prints nearby.

Using my limited powers of deduction, lack of experience, and an environmental assumption I started explaining how it was most likely a Leopard track. But I missed one crucial factor: it was too large to be a Leopard, and was a Lion print.

Kitsiso started pointing out some other vital clues about the print… the gait to the next print, the overlap of back feet to the front feet, and more.

It was a humbling moment, but a good one; the kind you learn from.

We moved on, and eventually saw and heard 33 species in the 3 hours, amongst some gorgeous scenery: large overhanging trees lapping up the abundantly clear water, water beetles skating on the surface, and large buffalo-coloured rocks and rich reeds competing for the edges.

The walk back was a bit more sedate, but still pretty, as our instructors always find a way to use a new route on the way back.

We got back to the vehicle in good time, and found out the other team had pranked us by removing one of the tyres. 😅
It proved to be a good opportunity to test my new tyre-replacing skills.

I've had 2 other guiding sessions since then, and still making mistakes, but each time it feels like I have had good teaching moments.
