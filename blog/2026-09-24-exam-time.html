--- title: Exam time and the wild hunt summary: Putting pen to paper, like it's 1997. tag: post date: 2026-09-24 ---

Today we started the first of our exams… an open book theory test, taking 1.5 hours, testing our knowledge of Geology, Ecology, Astronomy, Weather and climate, Reptiles, and Mammals.

We had a week to prepare and pour over our workbooks, read through our FGASA learning manuals and do the research to fill the gaps.

For the rest of the following week, we'll be doing more species ID practice, and writing our full theory exams.

The end of the usual morning and evenings

Our driving and walking sessions have stopped, and with it the early-moring routine of waking up at 4:30am.

My last drive went much better than before, and I was told if I keep up the pace, I should be able to pass my practical assessment. That's so reassuring.

The day after my last drive it was Rogan's turn and we all stopped for sundowners at Cheetah Dam, like we had many times before.
But this time was different.

The hunt

We saw a Wild Dog run across the opposite bank, my second sighting of the tireless canines.

And then more ran across in the same direction. Within a minute or so, we heard the death cries of a antelope… and Brian shouted: That's a kill! Let's go see!

We hurredly packed up the coolerboxes and junped into the two LandCruisers, abandoning caution and scurrying along the roads towards the sounds of the dogs yipping and cackling.

At first, all we could see was the flurry of white tail tips above the tall grass as the feeding frenzy ensued.

About 6 adult Wild Dogs and two juveniles ripped through the Duiker carcass in a few minutes.

We drove round the side of the sighting to get a better view, and saw the dogs toying with some bones and the fleshless head of the Duiker. The younger dogs were begging their parents for more to eat.

Some of them started breaking off and headed towards the main road… straight into the path of a rather large male Lion, and some accompanying onlookers in their various vehicles.

The Lion gave chase, causing the Dogs to drop their prized Duiker head, but they were far too quick and agile for the Lion.

The road was a mess of construction vehicles, contractors, staff, and other game viewing vehicles. It was clear that not everyone was thinking about the ethics of leaving their headlights on, instead maybe favouring their possible social media clout and trying to get illuminated photos/videos of the animals, subsequently burdened or benefitted by the artifical light.
