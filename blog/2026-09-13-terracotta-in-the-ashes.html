--- title: Terracotta in the ashes summary: Shadows of past industry tag: post date: 2026-09-13 ---

It's been a week of grinding (with some changes), and tomorrow I have to interpret the next set of activities.
But before I talk about those changes, there's something interesting I want to share first…

We've been seeing these really interesting patterns in some of the charred remains of the bushveld. Take a look at this photo and try think about what it is and what caused it:

Those dark/black regions are the carbon-rich remains of things like the grasses and small branches.
And the white strips are the concentrated mineral remains of the larger branches and trunk.
What do you think the russet or terracotta coloured areas are?

It reminds me of a crime scene. An outline of a corpse on the ground, its body position clearly outlined.

The whole scene is very fragile. The ashes get swept up in the wind, and they compress under our feet like stepping on a sponge.

Take a closer look…

Don't they remind you of small crumbly pieces of terracotta too?

Shadows of an industry

Let me connect the dots… There's a particular species here that likes to collect the cellulose from the decaying trees.

They then take that cellulose down into their underground chambers where they feed the masticated fibers to fungi, like an industrious group of farmers.

Being termites, they're quite vulnerable to predators, so they build protective tunnels to house their workers as they eat through the wood. And those tunnels are made out of clay and their saliva. And what we're seeing are the remnants of those termite tunnels - evidence of the remains of a working industry.
