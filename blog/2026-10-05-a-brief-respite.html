--- title: A brief respite summary: NQF2 done and dusted tag: post date: 2026-10-05 ---

That's it for the exams and assessments. We had a few cancellations and exaggerated anxiety as the weather turned foul for the first few assessments.

Thankfully, the weather cleared a day later and we could all get on with the tasks at hand, and in good time too.

I had a morning drive, and I was feeling a lot more confident, which showed in my score: 89%.
I missed a few chances to call out some of the bird calls and some easy interpretations to breach the 90% scores, but I was task focused, and not as opportunistic as I should have been.

Huge waves of relief ran through my student posse and I as we each ticked off our drives and got our results shortly after.

The last few days were relaxed, as we squared away the left over admin, went on sunset drives, and played some card games to unwind.

This morning we said our goodbyes to our assessors, instructors, and camp staff, a few tears were shed, and then we piled into the bush bus. Only a few kilometers out of the main Timbavati gate, and we saw a Leopard behind a bush fence on the side of the road, as if it made a special appearance to say goodbye just like the last Leopard did in Mashatu over a month ago.

A welcome break ahead

I'm currently sitting at Hoedspruit Airport, waiting for my flight back to Cape Town, for a week or so of relaxation.
It's also a chance to complete my Professional Driving Permit, so I can be better prepared for 2027's 6-month apprenticeship period.

Ayden (a fellow student of mine) will then pick me up on the 13th, and together we'll drive the long trek back (~1,800km) to be in Karongwe on the 14th.
