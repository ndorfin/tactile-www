--- title: Return to the wild summary: Changing tack completely, and immersing myself in nature tag: post date: 2026-08-03 ---

It's been 2 days on EcoTraining's Professional Field Guide course, a course that will have a stringent 6 months of study for the FGASA-approved NQF2 qualification, followed by a 5-month placement or internship program.

11 of us have come from all over the world to immerse ourselves in nature, and maybe even turn it into a career.

Everyone has been warmly welcoming, and my fellow students (while a lot younger than me) are a lively and positive bunch.

We're in Pridelands; a small reserve 5 minutes outside of Hoedspruit. It's rather dry, and mostly cold at night, but we've all come well prepared thanks to our excellent coordinators at EcoTraining.

The camp is situated alongside one of the only water reservoirs in the reserve, so it feels like all the animals come to us as they refresh themselves each day.

We've been on two game drives so far, and already seen some amazing things. Elephants sparring near the waterhole, Lions devouring a Buffalo, and being awoken in the middle of the night to the roar of the same well-fed pride.

The next few days

Today, we're learning about First Aid with a bush slant. As custodians to our future guests we're expected to look after them, even in the case of emergency. And over the next few months we'll learn other practical skills such as rifle handling, radio communication, driving off-road vehicles, and keeping the camps running smoothly and comfortably.

But that's nothing compared to the mountain of theory that awaits us in the books and the makeshift lecture hall, where we'll be spending the majority of our daylight hours. We'll be building knowledge in many of the expected disciplines, but also some you might not expect, such as:

  • Astronomy
  • Geology
  • Meteorology

Having that theoretical foundation will make us more informative, safe, and entertaining guides for our future visitors and customers, and I'm looking forward to learning it all, even the botany. 😉
